\section{Overview of the study}
\label{sec:overview}
% v0 DRAFT 18 Aug — for review.

The study has one design and asks it three questions in turn (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). A single pre-trained ECG foundation model is adapted, with a frozen backbone and small trainable adapters, to a synthetic cohort generated by a cardiac digital twin and to a real clinical cohort; from the adapted encoders a 1024-dimensional latent representation is exported for every recording, and alongside it a set of 54 hand-crafted ECG features (plus the frontal QRS axis) is extracted from the same signals as a clinically interpretable competitor. The first question is whether the two latent distributions can be brought together (\S\ref{sec:res-align}). The second is whether the simulator's mechanistic parameters, in particular the territory of a simulated infarct, can be read off the latent, in-domain and across the domain gap, judged by a decision rule fixed before the numbers existed (\S\ref{sec:res-decode}). The third question, which the first two make necessary, is \emph{why} the cross-domain readout falls short of its in-domain performance. The informativeness-fidelity audit measures, feature by feature, how much territory information the twin writes into each ECG channel compared with the real cohort (\S\ref{sec:res-audit}). Its answer is then tested as a prediction of which channels transfer (\S\ref{sec:res-predict}), and against every downstream repair that was tried (\S\ref{sec:res-repair}).

The contribution of this chapter is the experimental programme itself and the instruments built for it: the audit (\S\ref{sec:audit-method}) and the floor-aware evaluation of angular readouts (\S\ref{sec:p2}). To our knowledge (on the basis of the literature searches described in Chapter~\ref{ch:background}, with the usual caveat that such searches are bounded), no prior work reports a per-feature label-informativeness comparison of a mechanistic physiological simulator against a real cohort at sub-pathology resolution, links it to transfer prediction, or reports a zero-parameter clinical baseline against a foundation-model latent across the synthetic-to-real gap. Simulation-trained, real-tested localisation exists for other tasks, and in-silico infarct inverse inference exists without the real-cohort step (\S\ref{ch:background}). \tracenote{main report Part D.1; decision doc §3 novelty fences A–F}

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

Two ECG cohorts are used throughout: a synthetic cohort generated by a cardiac digital twin, \medalcare, and a real clinical cohort, \ptbxl. Both are public. This section fixes the counts, label spaces and pre-processing conventions that every later chapter relies on, including the validation checks the loading pipeline must pass (\S\ref{sec:preproc}).

\subsection{MedalCare-XL: the synthetic cohort}
\label{sec:data-medalcare}

\medalcare~\citep{gillette2023medalcare} is a set of 12-lead ECGs computed from anatomically and electrophysiologically parameterised whole-heart simulations. The release used here contains 16,839 ten-second recordings sampled at 500\,Hz in standard lead order (I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF, V1--V6), each carrying exactly one of eight pathology labels: sinus rhythm, myocardial infarction (MI), right and left bundle-branch block, left atrial enlargement, interatrial conduction block, fibrotic atrial cardiomyopathy, and AV block. Of the three signal variants shipped per simulation (raw, noise-augmented, filtered) only the filtered variant (0.5--150\,Hz Butterworth) is used. \tracenote{A§1.1.1; MedalCare-XL/WP2_largeDataset_Noise/README.txt; scripts/prepare_medalcare.py:112}

The dataset's own training/validation/test partition (${\approx}70/15/15$) is defined by the anatomical model with which the QRST complexes were simulated, so that ECGs from the same anatomy, with different electrophysiological parameters, fall into one partition only. This project inherits that partition unchanged: the working manifest reads the split off the release's directory names, and agreement with the directory structure is 100\,\% (16,839/16,839 rows). \tracenote{A§1.1.5, A§7.1; scripts/add_medalcare_splits.py; data/medalcare_filtered_manifest_dataset_split.csv} Table~\ref{tab:medalcare} gives the resulting counts. The MI subset is the cohort on which every $\thetavec$-decoding and territory experiment is run: 7,797 recordings across eight occlusion folders that combine the culprit artery (LAD, LCX, RCA), a transmurality parameter ($0.3$ or $1.0$) and, for LCX, an anterior or posterior sub-location. \tracenote{A§1.1.4; data/theta_mi_build_summary.json→territory_8c_counts}

\paragraph{The simulation parameters $\thetavec$.}
\label{sec:theta}
For each MI simulation the release ships the ischaemia parameters used to generate it. Four are read into the analysis: the angular position of the ischaemic region around the left ventricle, $\phiang \in (-\pi, \pi]$; its apico-basal position $z \in [0.10, 1.00]$; its size (75--175 in the simulator's units); and its transmural extent $\rho_{\varepsilon,\max} \in \{0.3, 1.0\}$. A fifth stored array, \texttt{transmural}, is a byte-identical duplicate of $\rho_{\varepsilon,\max}$ on all three splits and is not a separate parameter. \tracenote{A§1.1.6; data/theta_mi_*.npz; scripts/build_medalcare_isch_targets.py:71,308-311} Two properties of $\thetavec$ matter later. First, $z$ and size have identical ranges in every occlusion folder, so within the MI cohort the parameters that carry territory information are $(\phiang, \rho_{\varepsilon,\max})$ alone. Second, the four-class territory label used throughout, \territory, is defined from $\phiang$ by fixed wedges ($[0, 2]$\,rad $\to$ \AS, $(2, \pi] \to$ \AL, $[-2, 0) \to$ \INF, $[-\pi, -2) \to$ \IL) and not from the folder name. The two agree everywhere except in one folder, \texttt{LCX\_0.3\_post}, whose simulated angles fall in the anterolateral wedge; the build script relabels those rows and fails on any other disagreement. \tracenote{A§1.1.7; scripts/build_medalcare_isch_targets.py:143-168,350-387} Because \texttt{LCX\_0.3\_ant} and \texttt{LCX\_0.3\_post} occupy the same $\phiang$ wedge (the same distribution in all four parameters under two folder names), any readout of the \emph{folder} labels has a structural ceiling (macro-F$_1$ 0.864, with \IL\ recall pinned at 0.5). Results obtained before this was understood used the folder labels and were implicitly compared against 1.0; every four-class result in this thesis uses the $\phiang$-derived label, which has no such ceiling. \tracenote{A§1.1.7 :135-139; reports/2026-08-10_repo_audit_and_rerun_plan.md:140-153; dossier B §4.4}

The circular means of $\phiang$ within the four wedges, $+57.3^\circ$ for \AS, $+147.3^\circ$ for \AL, $-147.3^\circ$ for \IL and $-57.3^\circ$ for \INF, are the \emph{anchor angles} used whenever a real-domain territory label has to be placed on the simulator's circle. They are a construction of the simulator's own labelling (they reproduce the wedge midpoints to $0.04^\circ$), not an independent measurement. \tracenote{A§1.1.8; outputs/analysis/circular_geometry/floor_audit.json→anchors_deg}

\subsection{PTB-XL: the real cohort}
\label{sec:data-ptbxl}

\ptbxl\ v1.0.3 \citep{wagner2020ptbxl,strodthoff2021deep} contains 21,799 ten-second 12-lead ECGs from 18,869 patients, distributed with an official ten-fold patient-stratified partition. The 500\,Hz high-resolution files are used, so both domains enter the encoder as $(12 \times 5000)$ arrays. Each record carries SCP diagnostic statements grouped into five superclasses (NORM, MI, STTC, HYP, CD); labels are built multi-hot over every listed statement without a likelihood threshold. \tracenote{A§1.2.1-1.2.2; scripts/datasets.py:551-709}

\paragraph{The shared three-class space.}
Fine-tuning that sees both domains needs a common label space. The one used from Experiment~7 onwards is $\{$NORM, MI, CD$\}$: on the synthetic side sinus $\to$ NORM, MI $\to$ MI, and the two bundle-branch blocks, interatrial block and AV block $\to$ CD, with left atrial enlargement and fibrotic atrial cardiomyopathy dropped; on the real side NORM and MI map to themselves and the CD superclass to CD, with STTC and HYP dropped for want of a clean correspondence. \tracenote{A§1.2.3; scripts/finetune_multilabel.py:50-84} An earlier version of the real-side filter retained STTC in place of CD; classification metrics computed with it (Appendix~\ref{app:supp}) are on a different subset from those in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} and are not compared with them. \tracenote{A§7.7; A§1.2.3}

\paragraph{Infarct territory in the real cohort.}
Fourteen SCP statements belong to the MI superclass. They are mapped to the same four territories as the simulator by a fixed rule set. Anterior-side codes (AMI, ASMI, INJAS) give \AS, unless an anterolateral or lateral code (ALMI, INJAL, LMI, INJLA) is also present, in which case \AL. Inferior-side codes (IMI, INJIN) give \INF, unless an inferolateral or lateral code (ILMI, INJIL, IPLMI, LMI, INJLA) is present, in which case \IL. Records with any posterior code (PMI, IPMI), with both anterior- and inferior-side codes, or with lateral codes only are excluded. \tracenote{A§1.2.4; scripts/build_ptbxl_mi_subclass.py:88-159} Table~\ref{tab:ptbxl} gives the resulting cohort. Two evaluation sets appear in the results: fold~10 alone ($n=438$), the only legitimate test set for encoders whose training saw folds 1--9; and all ten folds ($n=4{,}324$, 3,794 patients), usable only for the encoder that never took a PTB-XL gradient step (\S\ref{sec:encoder}). An older three-class rule (single-territory Anterior/Inferior/Lateral) gives 444 rows on fold~10; whenever a count is quoted the rule is named. \tracenote{A§1.2.5, A§7.10; data/ptbxl_mi_subclass_summary.json; data/ptbxl_mi_subclass_allfolds.csv}

\subsection{Pre-processing and validation of the loading pipeline}
\label{sec:preproc}

Both loaders return $(12 \times 5000)$ arrays at 500\,Hz in the standard order I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF, V1--V6, obtained by re-indexing the WFDB header's \texttt{sig\_name} rather than by position, and z-scored per lead; the shared three-class filter keeps CD and drops STTC and HYP on the real side; and evaluation code reorders predicted-probability columns to the label order before any AUC is computed. \tracenote{A§1.1.9, A§1.2.2, A§1.3, A§7.7} Because the two domains come from different file formats and the synthetic side is used to fit readouts that are then applied to the real side, the pipeline is validated by checks that do not depend on any downstream result. The load-bearing one is physical: on the loaded signals the limb-lead identities $\mathrm{aVL} = (\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{III})/2$ and $\mathrm{aVF} = (\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{III})/2$ must hold for channels~4 and~5 respectively, in both domains; they do. \tracenote{A§1.3; reports/2026-08-10_lead_order_bug_diagnostic.md; dossier C Card A2} Earlier iterations of the synthetic loader did not pass these checks: one declared its input order with aVL and aVF transposed, and it applied a single global-scalar z-score; an earlier real-side filter kept the wrong superclass, as noted above. Every result reported in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} was produced with the validated pipeline and with encoders trained on it (the validated encoder family, \S\ref{sec:encoder}); results obtained with the earlier iterations appear only in Appendix~\ref{app:supp}, marked as such, and the earlier code state is preserved at a tagged commit of the public repository (\S\ref{sec:decl-data}). Two consequences of the loader corrections are themselves reported as findings in \S\ref{sec:res-align}: what a channel transposition does and does not do to cross-domain transfer, and how far the standard domain-classifier diagnostic is from detecting it. \tracenote{reports/2026-08-10_repo_audit_and_rerun_plan.md §0, §2; dossier C Card A}

\paragraph{Territory as an angle.}
\label{sec:angle}
Because the simulator's territory is a continuous angle while the real cohort's is a four-way diagnosis, the two are compared on the circle by assigning each real record the anchor angle of its territory (\S\ref{sec:theta}). Every real-domain ``angle'' in this thesis is therefore a four-point quantisation of the simulator's labelling, not a measurement; this is what makes a constant-predictor floor for circular agreement unavoidable and is why \S\ref{sec:pipelines} defines one. \tracenote{A§7.11; analysis/geom_common.py:158-177}

\paragraph{Anatomy models and splits.}
The release's promise that an anatomical model never spans partitions holds for 11 of the 13 model directories; two (\texttt{run\_S64}, \texttt{run\_S67}) straddle a partition boundary. In-domain cross-validation in this thesis groups by simulation run, which is finer than the anatomical model, so this is recorded as a limitation (\S\ref{ch:conclusion}) rather than corrected. \tracenote{A§7.2}

\section[The foundation model and its adaptation]{The foundation model and its adaptation}
\label{sec:encoder}
% v0 DRAFT 18 Aug — for review. Setup numbers trace to dossier A §2 (A§2.x) → net1d.py / finetune_model.py / scripts/finetune_multilabel.py / outputs/<run>/{args,metrics}.json.

\subsection{ECGFounder}
All encoders in this thesis are the same pre-trained network, \ecgfounder~\citep{li2024ecgfounder}, a one-dimensional residual convolutional network (Net1D) trained by its authors on over ten million 12-lead recordings. The configuration used here is the authors' 12-lead release: seven stages with filter widths $[64,160,160,400,400,1024,1024]$ and $[2,2,2,3,3,4,4]$ blocks per stage, kernel size 16, stride 2, grouped convolutions of width 16, no batch normalisation or dropout, applied to a $(12\times 5000)$ input. Global average pooling of the last stage gives a 1024-dimensional feature vector; this vector, taken \emph{before} any classification head, is what the thesis calls the latent representation $\mathbf{z}$ throughout. The pre-trained classification head is discarded and a fresh head attached for every fine-tuning run. \tracenote{A§2.1; finetune_model.py:23-62; net1d.py:367-420; checkpoint/12_lead_ECGFounder.pth}

\subsection{Adaptation: adapters and heads}
The backbone is frozen in every run. Adaptation happens through two small trainable components. \emph{Residual adapters} (\texttt{ConvAdapter1D}) are inserted at each stage: a $1\times1$ convolution down to $\max(8, C/16)$ channels, ReLU, a $1\times1$ convolution back up, added residually; the up-projection is zero-initialised so each adapter starts as an exact identity. \emph{Heads} are linear layers on the pooled feature (or, in the bottleneck experiments, a two-layer $\mathrm{Linear}(1024,K)\to\mathrm{GELU}\to\mathrm{Linear}(K,3)$ head, in which case the pre-GELU $K$-dimensional projection is the exported latent). In the shared-head runs the trainable parameter count is exactly 312{,}643 (adapters 309{,}568, head 3{,}075). \tracenote{A§2.1; net1d.py:98-115; scripts/finetune_bottleneck.py:77-94; outputs/_log_exp8_medalonly.txt:13} One implementation detail is recorded because it shaped the code: the model builder's ``linear probe with adapters'' path also freezes the head, so every shared-head run instead freezes the whole model, re-enables the adapters, and then re-enables the head explicitly.\footnote{\texttt{finetune\_model.py:52-58}; the workaround is at \texttt{scripts/}\allowbreak\texttt{finetune\_multilabel.py:1364-1375}.} Optimisation is Adam with the head at $10^{-3}$ and the adapters at $10^{-5}$, class-balanced binary cross-entropy (\texttt{pos\_weight} from the post-filter class balance), label smoothing 0.05, gradient clipping at 1.0, weight decay $10^{-5}$, batch size 128, a plateau learning-rate scheduler and early stopping; the checkpoint with the best validation score is kept. \tracenote{A§2.1, A§2.3.1 shared args.json fields}

\subsection{Fine-tuning modes}
Three regimes were used over the project, in the order they were introduced (Table~\ref{tab:runs}).
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{Single-domain}: adapters and a head on one dataset in its native label space (PTB-XL five superclasses; MedalCare eight classes).
\item \emph{Joint dual-head}: both datasets in alternating batches through the shared backbone and adapters, with a separate head per domain in its native label space, optionally with an MMD alignment loss between the two domains' pooled features. An early variant added a third, physics head regressing a 51-name vector of simulator parameters $\thetavec_{\mathrm{phys}}$ (activation-site and action-potential-duration settings; a different object from the four-member $\thetavec$ of \S\ref{sec:theta}); it was abandoned once an audit showed most of those coordinates to be dataset-wide constants or class tokens, and it plays no further role. \tracenote{A§2.2; net1d.py:429-511; dossier B §2.1 (physics head)}
\item \emph{Joint shared-head}: one three-class head over $\{$NORM, MI, CD$\}$ for both domains, labels remapped on the fly, one \texttt{pos\_weight} vector from the combined class balance, checkpoint selected on the mean of the two domains' validation macro-F$_1$. Optionally with a class-conditional MMD loss ($\lambda=0.1$): the unbiased RBF-kernel MMD$^2$ (median-heuristic bandwidth) computed per shared class and averaged over the classes present in both domains. A \emph{MedalCare-only} variant of the same code path takes no PTB-XL gradient step and selects its checkpoint on MedalCare validation alone; PTB-XL is scored each epoch but never enters the loss, the scheduler or checkpoint selection. \tracenote{A§2.2; scripts/finetune_multilabel.py:354-365,1336; losses/mmd.py:43-114}
\end{itemize}
The bottleneck heads ($K\in\{16,64,256\}$) and their multitask variant (a three-class head and a five-channel physics head on the shared $K$-dimensional projection, weighted by $\lambda_{\mathrm{cls}}$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{bio}}$) are trained on top of a frozen shared-head checkpoint. \tracenote{A§2.2; scripts/finetune_bottleneck_multitask.py}

\subsection{Which encoder carries the results}
The headline encoder for every cross-domain result is the MedalCare-only encoder (repository id \texttt{exp8\_leadfix\_medalonly}; Table~\ref{tab:runs}). It never took a PTB-XL gradient step and its checkpoint was chosen on MedalCare validation alone, so all ten PTB-XL folds are legitimate evaluation data ($n=4{,}324$ territory-labelled records, \S\ref{sec:data-ptbxl}); its PTB-XL classification score (0.457, Table~\ref{tab:exp8}) is a passive read-out and is reported only for completeness. Encoders that saw folds 1--9 in training (the dual-head encoder and its siblings) can be evaluated on fold~10 only ($n=438$); the dual-head encoder is the sensitivity arm. Because all encoders share one backbone, one adapter design and one seed, they are not independent replicates: the encoders are never counted as replicates, and where several are reported the effective number of independent observations is closer to two. \tracenote{A§1.2.5, A§2.3; CLAUDE.md active-state (medalonly, ~2 effective observations); decision doc §4 item 3}

\subsection{Latent export}
For each run and domain the pooled features are exported once, in the deterministic row order of the unshuffled split loader, to one archive per run and domain, \path{outputs/latents/<run>_<domain>/latents.npz} (\texttt{Z} the 1024- or $K$-dimensional latent, \texttt{P} the head outputs, \texttt{Y} the native labels; two later exports also carry the PTB-XL \texttt{ecg\_id}, against which the row-order convention was verified). All downstream analyses read these files; nothing after this point touches the network. \tracenote{A§3; scripts/export_latents.py:110-111,328-357; analysis/geom_common.py:115-127}

\section{The experimental programme}
\label{sec:programme}
% v0 DRAFT 18 Aug — for review. Sources: dossier B (definitions §2), dossier C (fact cards). Owner ruling 18 Aug: no dated timeline in the thesis — phases in logical order, no months.

\section{Hand-crafted ECG features and the control arm}
\label{sec:features}
% v0 DRAFT 18 Aug — for review. Setup numbers trace to dossier A §4.

Two hand-crafted feature sets are used, both extracted with NeuroKit2's discrete-wavelet delineator from the raw (un-normalised) voltages, with fiducial points detected once on lead~II and read at the same sample in every lead. \tracenote{A§4.1-4.2; scripts/extract_ecg_features_neurokit2.py:126-140; scripts/extract_ecg_features_spatial.py:42-46,110-121}
The six-feature set (\texttt{global6}) comprises QRS duration, QT interval, P duration, ST level 60\,ms after the J point averaged over V2--V6, T amplitude in lead~II, and heart rate. It has no spatial content and is used only as the historical baseline. The 54-feature set (\texttt{spatial54}) is a superset of it, built to be a fair competitor to the 1024-dimensional latent on a task that is spatial by definition: four per-lead measures in each of the twelve leads plus the six globals (Table~\ref{tab:features}). The four measures are the ST level 60\,ms after J (\STblock), the most negative sample between QRS onset and the R peak (\Qblock; pathological Q waves are the chronic-infarct localisation marker), the R-peak amplitude (\Rblock) and the T-peak amplitude (\Tblock). Reading all leads at the lead-II fiducial is deliberate: it samples the instantaneous QRS or T vector across the frontal and precordial planes, which per-lead re-delineation would misalign. \tracenote{A§4.2 :1-26,101-107,165-201} Two properties of the table matter for counting: \texttt{T\_amplitude\_mV} is an exact duplicate of \texttt{T\_amp\_II} in both domains (53 distinct columns), and \texttt{ST\_J60\_avg\_mV} is a V2--V6 average, not the mean of the twelve per-lead ST columns. \tracenote{A§4.2; main report C.1 data note} The \axisfeat, $\mathrm{atan2}(R_{\mathrm{aVF}}, R_{\mathrm{I}})$, is derived from two of these columns and treated as a 55th, circular, feature. \tracenote{A§4.3; analysis/fidelity_audit.py:81-84}

\section{Analysis pipelines}
\label{sec:pipelines}
% v0 DRAFT 18 Aug — for review. Definitions trace to dossier A §5 (code line pointers there).

Three analysis pipelines produce every number in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}. They are named here and referred to by name whenever a macro-F$_1$ is quoted, because they score different rows with different estimators and their numbers are not interchangeable (Table~\ref{tab:pipelines}).

\subsection{P1: the classifier pipeline}
\label{sec:p1}
The territory readout is a multinomial logistic regression (L2, balanced class weights) on the standardised latent or feature matrix, with the regularisation strength chosen by five-fold stratified cross-validation on the \emph{source} training rows over $C\in\{10^{-5},\dots,10^{2}\}$ and refit on all of them. Scoring reports macro-F$_1$ and balanced accuracy with 1,000-resample percentile bootstrap intervals and label-shuffle permutation $p$-values ($N=10{,}000$ refits for the logistic probes, so that the $p$-floor of $10^{-4}$ survives multiplicity correction); a two-class collapse (anterior = \AS+\AL, inferior = \INF+\IL) is scored from the same predictions without refitting. Five endpoints were fixed as the pre-registered family before any result was inspected, and Holm--Bonferroni is applied over that family with its size held fixed. \tracenote{A§5.1 :146,1184-1236,181-188,206-220,246+}

Two features of P1 carry weight later. First, its scaler modes for the cross-domain matrix are named by their code identifiers, because two of them have been called by the same informal name in the working record and they are not the same thing. \texttt{target}, the legacy mode, fits a fresh scaler on the label-selected PTB-XL evaluation rows themselves; it is transductive and unlabelled, but conditioned on the label-selected subset. \texttt{target\_pool\_measured} fits column-wise statistics on the full un-imputed PTB-XL split, so nothing label-dependent enters the scaler, and imputed rows stay out of the feature arm's statistics. \texttt{source} reuses the MedalCare scaler unchanged; the code documents it as a defect for the feature arm, since the two domains disagree on per-column spread by up to threefold, and it is not reported. Every P1 cell in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} names its mode. \tracenote{A§5.1 standardise_target :451-533; dossier C Card J} Second, arm-versus-arm comparisons use a paired test (\texttt{paired\_macro\_f1}): both arms are scored on the same resampled rows for a bootstrap interval on $\Delta = \mathrm{F1}_A - \mathrm{F1}_B$, and a per-row prediction swap gives a permutation $p$ for ``the two arms are interchangeable''. A permutation $p$ against an arm's own label-shuffle null certifies only that the arm uses the labels; at $n=4{,}324$ every such $p$ sits at its floor for every arm, and it is never quoted as evidence that one arm is better than another. \tracenote{A§5.1 :837-894; CLAUDE.md active-state}

\subsection{P2: the circular-geometry pipeline}
\label{sec:p2}
Territory is an angle in the simulator, so the second pipeline reads it out as one. A multi-output ridge maps the standardised latent (or features) to $(\cos\phiang, \sin\phiang)$, with the ridge parameter chosen by generalised cross-validation on the training rows over $\alpha\in[10^{-2},10^{5}]$; the design is SVD-factorised once so that label-permutation nulls (500 draws) are cheap. In-domain evaluation is five-fold cross-validation with folds disjoint by simulation run (MedalCare) or by patient (PTB-XL). Transport fits on all MedalCare rows and applies the readout to PTB-XL, never refitting, under two scalers, and both are always reported. \texttt{source} is the strict transport: MedalCare medians and moments, no target-domain information at all. \texttt{target} is diagonal CORAL \citep{sun2016coral}: PTB-XL medians and per-column re-standardisation mapped back through the source moments, a mild transductive step that uses unlabelled target statistics. \tracenote{A§5.2 geom_common.py:183-240,275-283; circular_geometry.py:93-170; block_transfer.py:222-235}

\paragraph{Scoring an angular readout, and the constant-predictor floor.}
Predicted angles are scored by three quantities. The circular mean resultant length of the errors, $\circR = |\mathrm{mean}\,e^{i(\hat\phi-\phi)}|$, is the natural agreement measure but has a floor that is easy to overlook: for any predictor that emits the same angle $c$ for every row, $\circR = |e^{ic}|\cdot|\mathrm{mean}\,e^{-i\phi}| = |\mathrm{mean}\,e^{-i\phi}|$; the constant cancels, and every constant predictor scores exactly the resultant length of the label marginal. That value is $\Rfloor = \Rfloorptb$ on the four-anchor PTB-XL target and $\Rfloormedal$ on the continuous MedalCare $\phiang$ (0.1227 on MedalCare quantised to four anchors), and it is the supremum over all label-independent predictors. A label-permutation null does not substitute for it: permutation preserves the marginal and destroys only the pairing, so its expected resultant vector is approximately $\circR_{\mathrm{pred}}\cdot\Rfloor \le \Rfloor$. The shuffle null therefore sits \emph{below} the constant floor, and ``beats its own shuffle null'' is strictly weaker than ``beats a constant''. Every $\circR$ in this thesis therefore appears with its floor. \tracenote{A§5.2 floor_audit.py:9-49,86-104; floors from outputs/analysis/circular_geometry/floor_audit.json→floors} Because $\circR$ can also be won by a well-placed constant, two floor-free metrics are reported alongside it: nearest-anchor macro-F$_1$, obtained by assigning each predicted angle to the nearest of the four territory anchors (its own constant floor is the best single-class predictor, \Ffloorptb\ on PTB-XL and \Ffloormedal\ on MedalCare), and circular $\etasq$, the between-territory share of angular dispersion (zero for any constant). \tracenote{A§5.2 block_transfer.py:141-188}

\paragraph{The two mandatory nulls and the axis baseline.}
Any claim that a particular latent direction or subspace is special is tested against two nulls, each with the whole downstream pipeline re-run: a \emph{norm-matched random projection} (the learned coefficient matrix replaced by a Gaussian matrix of the same shape and per-column norm) and a \emph{shuffled-source-label refit} (MedalCare $\phiang$ permuted at run level, the readout refitted with a fresh $\alpha$, transported and scored). Uncertainty is by block bootstrap (simulation runs in MedalCare, patients in PTB-XL), and arm-versus-arm differences use group-bootstrap intervals and group-swap permutations on identical resamples. Finally, a zero-parameter clinical baseline is carried through every cross-domain analysis: the \axisfeat\ assigned to its nearest MedalCare anchor, with no fitting at all. \tracenote{A§5.2 channel_repair.py:425-486,166-185; block_transfer.py:302+; main report A.3}

\subsection{Alignment diagnostics}
\label{sec:align-method}
The distance between the two latent distributions is measured by a held-out classifier two-sample test \citep[C2ST: the AUROC of a domain classifier, linear or gradient-boosted, trained to tell synthetic from real latents;][]{lopezpaz2017c2st}, by the unbiased RBF-kernel MMD$^2$ \citep[median-heuristic and five-bandwidth variants;][]{gretton2012kernel}, and by $k$-nearest-neighbour domain mixing. Post-hoc linear alignment uses iterative nullspace projection \citep[INLP;][]{ravfogel2020null}: a balanced logistic domain classifier is fitted, its direction is projected out of both domains, and the step repeats until the domain accuracy falls to a stopping level or an iteration budget runs out. The aligned latents are then evaluated on held-out splits, once with a fitting pool that contains the PTB-XL test split and once symmetrically, with the pool disjoint from every test split. The lead-permutation sweep applies each of the 77 permutations (identity, all 66 transpositions, ten random permutations) to the PTB-XL test signals at inference only, with the territory readout fitted once on MedalCare and never refit. \tracenote{dossier B §2.3-2.4; dossier C Cards F, G; analysis/nonlinear_c2st.py; analysis/inlp_alignment.py; scripts/_t42_leadperm_sweep.py}

\paragraph{Controls that changed conclusions.}
Several controls in these pipelines exist because an earlier reading of the data was found to be an instrument error; they are listed here once, because they are part of the method and because the corrections they forced are the reason the reported numbers can be trusted. (i)~The limb-lead identity check on the loaded signals (\S\ref{sec:preproc}). (ii)~The constant-predictor floor for $\circR$, which showed that a transported readout scoring $\circR=0.26$ on PTB-XL is below the constant-predictor floor of $\Rfloorptb$; conclusions drawn from $\circR$ alone were withdrawn and replaced by floor-free metrics. (iii)~The random-projection and shuffled-label nulls, which showed that a supervised increment over the frontal-axis baseline was matched by arbitrary projections. (iv)~A non-linear C2ST, which showed that a linear domain classifier's fall towards chance after nullspace projection did not mean the domains had become indistinguishable; a linear probe licenses only claims about linear structure. (v)~The paired arm-versus-arm test, which replaced ``each arm beats its own shuffle null''. (vi)~The requirement that a cross-domain latent-versus-control comparison use an encoder that never saw the target domain, which reversed an earlier reading of that comparison. \tracenote{main report A.1-A.5; dossier C Cards D, E, G; CLAUDE.md active-state}

\section{The informativeness-fidelity audit}
\label{sec:audit-method}
% v0 DRAFT 18 Aug — for review.

The audit asks, feature by feature, how much information about infarct territory each ECG measurement carries in the synthetic cohort and how much in the real one. For every column of the 54-feature matrix and for the frontal axis, the between-territory share of variance is computed in each domain: ANOVA $\etasq$ against the four-class label (NaN-aware, so a missing value in one column never drops a row) and circular $\etasq$ for the axis. Uncertainty comes from a 500-draw block bootstrap (simulation runs in MedalCare, patients in PTB-XL), with the domains resampled independently and the sim--real difference paired by draw index; a 95\,\% interval for the difference that excludes zero classifies the feature as a \emph{blind spot} (informative in reality, flat in simulation) or a \emph{spurious channel} (informative in simulation, flat in reality). Marginal realism is measured separately for every feature by the standardised mean difference and the standard-deviation ratio between domains, so that distributional similarity and informativeness can be compared. Rank agreement between the two $\etasq$ profiles is summarised by Spearman's $\rho$ but tested with a block-aware permutation, because features within a measure-block are not independent observations; the licensed statement is therefore about blocks, not about the 54-feature ranking. The whole audit is repeated on the two-class collapse of the label as a granularity check. Every point estimate was reproduced by an independent re-implementation, whose corrections are the values reported. \tracenote{A§5.2 fidelity_audit.py:115-163,217-302,311+; main report C.1; verifier: analysis/fidelity_audit_verify.py}

\section[Transport, prediction and repair]{Transport, prediction and repair protocols}
\label{sec:transport-method}
% v0 DRAFT 18 Aug — for review. Q1b (P2 scaler primacy) is an open supervisor call; both P2 modes are always reported.

\paragraph{Does the audit predict transfer?}
For each feature block (\STblock, \Qblock, \Rblock, \Tblock, globals; all 54; the two-column axis pair) a P2 readout is fitted on MedalCare and scored in-domain (shared run-disjoint folds, so blocks are compared on identical rows) and after transport to PTB-XL under both scalers. Transfer efficiency is $(\mathrm{F1}_{\mathrm{cross}} - \Ffloorptb)/(\mathrm{F1}_{\mathrm{in}} - \Ffloormedal)$, i.e.\ the fraction of above-floor in-domain performance that survives transport. The prediction that the simulator's most informative blocks would transfer worst was written to disk before any transfer number was computed; the association between block informativeness and efficiency is tested with exact permutation over the five blocks (one- and two-sided both reported), and its sensitivity to the cross-validation fold seed is declared. \qone{Under the P2 modes, the prediction concerns raw transport (\texttt{source}); the \texttt{target} mode is reported as the boundary at which per-column recalibration changes it.} \tracenote{A§5.2 block_transfer.py:86-136,141-268,302+; main report C.2}

\paragraph{Can the transfer be repaired?}
Three interventions are tested, all fitted on MedalCare with PTB-XL features never used at inference. (A)~\emph{Channel restriction}: a ridge map from the latent to the 54 features is fitted on all-54-finite MedalCare rows; restricting it to a block gives a low-dimensional representation $r_{\mathrm{block}}(\mathbf z)$ on which the P2 readout is refitted, so a restricted readout uses only the latent directions that predict, say, the Q and R amplitudes. Restrictions tested: Q, R, ST and T (12-d each), Q+R (24-d), the axis pair, and two lead groups: inferior (II, III, aVF; four measures each, 12-d) and anterior (V1--V4, 16-d). Each restricted arm is compared with the unrestricted 1024-d readout by the paired group bootstrap on identical rows and tested against both mandatory nulls. (B)~\emph{Importance reweighting}: a logistic domain classifier on summary features gives weights $w = p_{\mathrm{real}}/(1-p_{\mathrm{real}})$ (clipped at the 99th percentile) with which MedalCare rows are resampled before refitting; the effective sample size $1/\sum w^2$ is reported, an ESS-matched uniform resample controls for the loss of rows, and the reweighted readout is compared with the unweighted one on the same rows. (C)~\emph{Recalibration}: the diagonal-CORAL \texttt{target} scaler of P2, already part of every transport table. Every arm was re-run by an independent implementation before being reported. \tracenote{A§5.2 channel_repair.py:157-253,425-486,508-577; main report C.3; verifier: analysis/channel_repair_verify.py}

\paragraph{Pre-registration and reporting rules.}
Three practices are applied throughout the results. Decision rules and predictions are written down before the numbers exist (the territory-decoding rule of \S\ref{sec:res-decode}; the prediction of \S\ref{sec:res-predict}) and are quoted verbatim when scored. Arm-versus-arm claims cite paired tests only. Cross-domain numbers appear under both scaler modes of their pipeline or not at all, and every $\circR$ appears with its constant floor.
