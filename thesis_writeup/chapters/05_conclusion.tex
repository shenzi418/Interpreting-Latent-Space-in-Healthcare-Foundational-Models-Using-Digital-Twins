\section{Findings}
\label{sec:conc-findings}

The registered question was whether the latent space of an ECG foundation model, adapted to a cardiac digital twin, can be interpreted in the twin's mechanistic parameters, and whether that interpretation carries to real ECGs. The findings, in the order the thesis established them and with the strength each measurement supports:

Within the twin's domain, the latent is interpretable, and richly so. Infarct territory is linearly readable from the frozen backbone at macro-F$_1$ 0.66, exceeding a 54-feature clinical control by $+0.15$ on identical rows ($p=10^{-4}$) and replicating at $+0.17$ on an independent readout, both measured in \S\ref{sec:indomain}. This is a measured result, and it is the half of the registered question that can be answered with an unqualified yes. \tracenote{all Ch5 numbers restated from Ch4 sections cited inline}

Across the gap, the answer is partial and precisely bounded. Under the decision rule fixed before any cross-domain number existed, a readout fitted only on the twin meets the two-class transfer bar under both scaler modes and misses the four-class bar under both, the verdict of \S\ref{sec:track3}. On the twelve paired tests of \S\ref{sec:grid12}, the latent and the control are statistically indistinguishable across the gap. What does transfer is compact: a zero-shot anchor frame, present under rescaled transport only, and a twelve-dimensional inferior-lead subspace at parity with the full readout, established in \S\ref{sec:floors} and \S\ref{sec:res-repair}.

The audit is the thesis's instrument, and its map, drawn in \S\ref{sec:res-audit}, is a discovery about the twin. \medalcare\ relocates territory information across measurement blocks. Its signal sits in the ST segment, acute injury physiology; the real cohort's sits in the Q and R waves and the frontal axis, the signature of established infarction. In total, 28 features plus the axis are confirmed blind spots and 14 columns are spurious, all with bootstrap intervals. Marginal realism and informativeness fidelity dissociate: the block with the worst marginals is the only informativeness-matched one. The clinical reading, an acute simulator meeting a chronic registry, is consistent with the map and is not established by it. The acuity test that could have established it does not support it.

The audit predicts, and \S\ref{sec:res-predict} is the test. Real-domain block informativeness ranks raw transport efficiency exactly. The twin's most informative block transports at the constant-predictor floor, and the informativeness-matched block transfers best after recalibration. Prediction validated out of sample is the strongest property an audit of this kind can have, and it is what separates the instrument from a post-hoc explanation.

The boundary is measured, and it has a mechanism; \S\ref{sec:res-align} and \S\ref{sec:res-repair} carry it. Restriction to faithful channels, importance reweighting and per-column recalibration all fail to recover the loss; reweighting actively harms. The two domains' territory readout planes are near-orthogonal in the latent space, even though local neighbourhood structure partially corresponds. The deficit behaves as information absent from the synthetic signal, not information misplaced in it. Alignment of the two latent distributions was not achieved by any method tried; the gap is over-determined, and the standard detector is blind even to gross input corruption.

The methodological contribution is the floor-aware evaluation of angular readouts: the constant-predictor floor as a supremum, the demonstration that permutation nulls sit below it, and the floor-free scores. Applied to this project's own earlier numbers, it reversed conclusions, which is the recommendation for it.

\section{What this means for synthetic data in cardiology}
\label{sec:conc-meaning}

The general lesson is the dissociation itself: how realistic a synthetic cohort looks and how much task information it carries are different properties, and only the second determines what a model trained on it can do elsewhere. \medalcare's own clinician study had already measured the symptom, 39\,\% diagnosability against 62\,\% for real recordings; the audit localises it, feature by feature, and shows it predicts downstream transfer. For anyone proposing to train on a mechanistic simulator, the practical prescription is cheap: before training anything, compute per-feature label-informativeness in the synthetic cohort and in a real cohort of the target population, and read the disagreement. It needs features and labels only, no encoder and no transfer experiment, and on these data it runs in minutes.

For simulator builders the map is a work order rather than a verdict. The twin's ST-segment physiology is doing what an acute ischaemia model should; what the real registry rewards is the electrophysiology of established infarction, Q-wave formation, R-wave loss and axis shift, written into the signal at the strength reality shows. A simulator corrected on exactly the channels the audit flags would give a transported readout something to carry, and the audit can be re-run to verify the correction. The pay-off is the promise of \S\ref{sec:intro-motivation}: interpretable readouts on real ECGs, and training signal for conditions registries cannot supply. Nothing in this thesis argues against digital twins; it argues for validating them on the information a task needs.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:conc-limits}

The scope of every claim is one simulator, one real cohort, one backbone family and one delineator. Within that scope, the specific limitations are these. The encoders share a backbone, an adapter design and a seed, so the effective number of independent encoder observations is nearer two than six, and no multi-seed replication of the fine-tuning runs exists. Uncertainty comes from patient- and run-block resampling instead. The audit's $\etasq$ is a linear-in-variance measure, and a rank-based or information-theoretic informativeness could order features differently; this was not run under the experimental freeze. The two label constructions differ in kind: simulated territory derives from a continuous angle, real territory from diagnosis codes. The out-of-sample transport tests and the two-class collapse answer the resulting objection, with one feature, the V6 R amplitude, flipping classification between granularities. Real-domain angles are assigned territory anchors, not measurements, which is what makes the constant-predictor floor unavoidable.

Which scaler mode is primary for transport claims remains a declared open question; every cross-domain number appears under both. The block-level transfer correlation rests on five blocks and is fold-seed fragile, which is why the paired orderings carry the claim. The inferior-lead parity cell is the best of eight tested restrictions and is reported under that selection. The simulated interval durations are missing not at random, and their simulated informativeness is flagged wherever quoted. Two anatomical models straddle the release's split boundary, and the in-domain cross-validation groups by simulation run, which is finer than the anatomical model. Finally, results from the earlier run families appear only in Appendix~\ref{app:prefix} and are never compared with main-text values. \tracenote{limitations list fixed in 02_writing_guide.md §5; each item traced in its section}

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:conc-future}

Four extensions follow directly and none was run here. A second simulator and a second real cohort would test whether the marginal-realism/informativeness dissociation generalises beyond this pair. A second backbone family would separate representation effects from encoder effects. A rank-based informativeness would remove the audit's linearity assumption. And an acute real cohort, where ST physiology should carry genuine territory information, would test the clinical reading of the map directly; there, the audit predicts the twin's transfer should improve. Each is a test the audit makes precise.

\section{Closing statement}
\label{sec:conc-close}

Adapted to a cardiac digital twin, a foundation model's latent space is interpretable in the twin's own mechanistic terms, down to the angular position of a simulated infarct. Carried to real ECGs, that interpretation survives only in part, and the part that fails does so for a reason this thesis measures rather than conjectures: the twin writes its territory information where reality does not read it. The instrument that found this is small, cheap and reusable, and its predictions held out of sample. Interpreting the latent space, it turned out, meant auditing the twin.
