\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:intro-motivation}

Two developments form the basis of this thesis. Cardiac digital twins can now produce realistic 12-lead ECGs at scale, with the physiology that generated each recording known exactly. The largest publicly available example is \medalcare, which includes 16,839 simulated ECGs across eight pathologies \citep{gillette2023medalcare}; for each simulated infarct, the location, extent and transmurality that caused it are known. Foundation models for the ECG, trained on millions of clinical recordings, give the field reusable representations that can be applied to new tasks with only minor adaptation \citep{li2024ecgfounder}. Together these suggest a clinically useful move: adapt a foundation model to a twin, learn to read the twin's mechanistic parameters out of the model's latent space, and then apply that readout to patients. The outcome would be estimates, infarct territory among them, that no clinical label set could have supervised directly. A readout expressed in a simulator's mechanistic parameters is physiologically interpretable in a way that a black-box risk score is not. And a simulator can provide unlimited labelled examples of conditions too rare for any registry to teach, which is precisely the small-data regime where transfer from synthetic cohorts would matter most. Throughout, standard deep-learning concepts, including convolutional networks, learned representations and fine-tuning, are used in their textbook sense \citep{goodfellow2016deep}.

The programme is based on an assumption that is generally not stated explicitly: that a synthetic cohort verified as realistic can be used as a substitute for real data. The validation carried out for \medalcare\ is genuine and careful, distributional overlays of ECG features against a real reference and a clinician study run by the authors themselves, and it is validation of a particular kind: it checks how the data \emph{looks}. Whether a synthetic cohort carries the \emph{information} a downstream task needs is a different matter, and the authors' own clinician study hints that the two can come apart: cardiologists diagnosed the pathology of real ECGs 62\,\% of the time, and of simulated ones only 39\,\% \citep{gillette2023medalcare}. No published work says where in the signal that missing information lives.

The stakes reach past this one simulator. If realism checks can pass while task information is missing, then every simulator-trained readout inherits a silent failure mode, and validation practice for synthetic medical data needs an instrument the realism checks do not provide. This thesis measures that failure mode on one twin, one real cohort and one foundation model, explains it at the level of individual ECG measurements, and tests the explanation.

\section{The question}
\label{sec:intro-question}

As registered, the project asks: can the latent space of an ECG foundation model, adapted to a cardiac digital twin, be interpreted in terms of the twin's mechanistic parameters, and does that interpretation carry over to real ECGs? The parameters in question are those of \medalcare's simulated infarcts, above all the angular position $\phiang$ of the ischaemic region, which induces a four-class infarct territory expressible in both the synthetic cohort and, through diagnosis codes, in the real cohort \ptbxl\ \citep{wagner2020ptbxl}.

The answer developed over the year has three parts. In-domain, yes: territory is linearly readable from the latent, far beyond what a 54-feature clinical control extracts from the same recordings. Across the gap, partially: under a decision rule fixed before any cross-domain number existed, the two-class endpoint (anterior versus inferior) is met and the four-class endpoint is not, and the latent's large in-domain advantage over the control disappears entirely. The third part is the question the first two force: \emph{why} does an advantage that large fail to travel? The thesis's central contribution answers it with an instrument: a per-feature audit of how much territory information each standard ECG measurement carries in the twin against the real cohort. The audit explains the shortfall, and it predicts, before any transfer is run, which parts of the signal survive it.

The method is one design asked three questions (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). One frozen foundation model is adapted to both cohorts through a validated loading pipeline. Its latent representation and a set of 54 hand-crafted features are extracted from the same recordings. The analyses then ask, in order: can the two latent distributions be aligned; can territory be decoded within and across domains; and where does each domain write its territory information.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro-contributions}

The thesis makes six empirical contributions and one methodological one. Each is stated here with the section that carries it; the strength of each claim is fixed where it is made.

\tracenote{contribution numbers restated from Ch4, where each carries its artifact trace}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \emph{The latent is interpretable in the twin's parameters, in-domain} (\S\ref{sec:indomain}). Infarct territory is decodable from the frozen backbone's 1024-dimensional latent at macro-F$_1$ 0.66, exceeding a 54-feature clinical control by $+0.15$ on identical rows ($p=10^{-4}$), replicated at $+0.17$ on an independent readout.
\item \emph{A pre-registered transfer endpoint is met, a finer one is not} (\S\ref{sec:track3}). A territory readout fitted only on the twin sorts real infarcts into anterior versus inferior above its pre-registered bar, under both scaler modes. The four-class endpoint is not met, and across the gap the latent and the control are statistically indistinguishable on twelve paired tests (\S\ref{sec:grid12}).
\item \emph{What transfers is compact and identifiable} (\S\ref{sec:floors}, \S\ref{sec:res-repair}). The transferable content amounts to a zero-shot anchor frame and a twelve-dimensional inferior-lead subspace that matches the full 1024-dimensional readout across the gap.
\item \emph{An informativeness-fidelity audit of the twin, and a discovery made with it} (\S\ref{sec:res-audit}). Feature by feature, the twin relocates territory information across measurement blocks: its territory signal sits in the ST segment, while the real cohort's sits in the Q and R waves and the frontal QRS axis, with 28 confirmed blind spots and 14 spurious channels. Marginal realism does not predict any of it.
\item \emph{The audit predicts transfer} (\S\ref{sec:res-predict}). Real-domain informativeness ranks raw transport efficiency exactly; the twin's most informative block transports at the constant-predictor floor; the one informativeness-matched block transfers best after recalibration.
\item \emph{The boundary, with its mechanism} (\S\ref{sec:res-align}, \S\ref{sec:res-repair}). The two latent distributions are not aligned by any method tried, and the gap is over-determined; channel restriction, reweighting and recalibration do not recover the cross-domain loss. The deficit behaves as missing information, not misplaced information.
\item \emph{Floor-aware evaluation of angular readouts} (\S\ref{sec:p2}, \S\ref{sec:floors}). The constant-predictor floor of circular agreement on a non-uniform target, stated as a supremum; the demonstration that permutation nulls sit below it; and the floor-free scores that replace it. Applied throughout, these reversed more than one of the project's own preliminary conclusions.
\end{enumerate}

Two disciplines run through all of it. Every cross-domain number appears under both scaler modes of its pipeline, because which mode is primary is a genuinely open methodological question, declared as such (\S\ref{sec:transport-method}); and every arm-versus-arm claim rests on a paired test, never on comparison of separate significance levels.

\section{Thesis outline}
\label{sec:intro-outline}

Chapter~\ref{ch:background} gives the background: infarct electrocardiography, the twin and the real cohort, foundation models, inverse inference, domain adaptation, the evaluation of synthetic data, circular statistics, and the register the thesis is written in. Chapter~\ref{ch:contribution} presents the data, models, pipelines and instruments, including the audit and the transport and repair protocols, together with the validated loading pipeline and the programme of experiments actually run. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} contains the results in the order the questions arose: adaptation, the domain gap, decoding within and across domains, the audit, its predictions, and the limits of repair. Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} places the findings against the claims, draws the implications for validating synthetic cohorts, and states the limitations. Chapter~\ref{ch:declarations} contains the required declarations, including a full disclosure of generative-AI use in this project.
