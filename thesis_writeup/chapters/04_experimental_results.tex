The MedalCare-only row frames everything that follows. Four families of alignment attempt end at the same value. Training-time MMD, class-agnostic and class-conditional, reduces its own loss statistic by 8--13\,\% and leaves held-out C2ST at 1.0; post-hoc nullspace projection is treated in the next paragraph; projecting the latents to as few as eight principal components leaves a non-linear C2ST above 0.99; and a bottleneck trained end-to-end at $K=16$ behaves the same. These are appendix material individually (Appendix~\ref{app:prefix}); their joint content is that first-order distances, linear structure and dimensionality can each be manipulated freely without the two distributions becoming confusable. \tracenote{dossier B §2.3-2.4; Card G withdrawn dim_scan readings (GBDT 0.9948-0.9968); EXEC LOG}

The nullspace-projection episode is reported in detail because it produced the chapter's methodological rule. Iteratively removing the directions a linear domain classifier finds drives the held-out \emph{linear} C2ST from 1.0000 to 0.5001, chance, and the first reading of that number was that the domains had been aligned at a cost in transfer. The reading was withdrawn when the detector was changed: on the same projected latents a gradient-boosted classifier reads 0.9987--0.9999. Instrument and intervention shared a blind spot, since both were linear. With a non-linear detector on the axis there is no alignment--transfer trade-off to negotiate: the C2ST frontier is flat from zero to 160 removed directions while transfer falls to chance, and a rank-matched random projection holds transfer near 0.70 throughout, so the fall is specific, not capacity loss. The rule this fixed, applied throughout the thesis, is that a linear probe licenses only claims about linear structure. \tracenote{Card G items 2-4 (0.5001; GBDT 0.9987/0.9999; flat frontier; random control 0.70; specificity gap +0.4999)}

The gap is over-determined. Destroying the dependence structure of the latents while keeping every marginal leaves a non-linear classifier at 0.9999; destroying every marginal while keeping the dependence structure leaves it at 0.9993; destroying both drops it to 0.5032, confirming the floor behaves. This holds on all six checkpoints down to $K=16$, where the sharpest single contrast in the project sits: after nullspace projection the linear C2ST reads 0.5808 while a gradient-boosted classifier reads 0.9968 from the dependence structure alone, with every marginal destroyed. There is no single defect to remove; marginals and dependence each suffice to give the domains away. A label-shift explanation is separately excluded by a lower-bound argument, and the directions the projection removes carry no clinical or biophysical signal (every excess $R^2$ against the hand-crafted features and against $\thetavec$ is negative); the details are in Appendix~\ref{app:prefix}. \tracenote{Card G item 6 (domain_mechanism_replication: 0.9999/0.9993/0.5032, 6/6; K16 0.5808 vs 0.9968) and item 8 (label-shift bound slack +0.073; excess R2 all negative)}

If the detector is this sensitive, does it at least detect things that matter? The loading configurations of Table~\ref{tab:threecond} give one instrument test. On the same frozen encoder, territory transfer under standard loading scores 0.328 against 0.213 with transposed leads and global z, with the two mixed configurations falling in between and the interaction carrying the right sign; the movement is consistent with the standard order being the right one, and it is not treated as proof of it, for the reason the next experiment supplies. \tracenote{lead-order diagnostic §2-4 (0.2132 p=0.756 / 0.3278 p=0.0020, involution 2x2 on frozen exp7_baseline; P1-style probe)}

The 77-cell sweep asks the question systematically: apply the identity, all 66 pairwise lead transpositions, and ten random full permutations to the real test signals at inference, with the territory probe fitted once on the simulator and never refit, and watch what each diagnostic does (Table~\ref{tab:sweep}). Transfer spans a factor of two across cells (macro-F$_1$ 0.162--0.320), and across that range the linear C2ST moves by $10^{-5}$ and the gradient-boosted C2ST by $9\times10^{-5}$, correlating with damage at $\rho = -0.04$ and $-0.03$: a detector pinned at its ceiling ranks a physiological domain gap and a channel-swapped recording identically. Transfer itself detects gross corruption (seven of ten random permutations fall below the identity's confidence interval) and misses fine corruption entirely: not one of the 66 transpositions falls below the identity's interval, 39 of 66 score above the correctly ordered cell, and the transposition that the historical loader defect implemented ranks 70th of 77 by damage. The kernel distance separates random permutations from transpositions cleanly but does not track harm within transpositions ($\rho=-0.15$, $p=0.20$): it flags change, not damage. Two conclusions carry forward. Cross-domain transfer is a corruption detector, not a lead-order detector, which is why the lead-order convention rests on the physical identity check (Appendix~\ref{app:supp}) and not on the transfer movement above; and every diagnostic in routine use here is blind to something a reader would assume it sees. \tracenote{leadperm_sweep.json + Card F (spread 0.0929; C2ST spreads 1e-5/9e-5; rho -0.040/-0.031; randoms 7/10 below CI [0.2162,0.3011], 0/10 p<0.05; 0/66 below, 39/66 above; historical cell rank 70/77; MMD rho -0.146 p=0.20; identity p=0.1139)}

One distribution-level intervention does buy something, and naming what it buys sets up the rest of the chapter. Per-column re-standardisation of the latents on unlabelled target statistics, diagonal CORAL, recovers $+0.065 \pm 0.020$ of cross-domain class transfer across checkpoints and seeds, saturating with fifty unlabelled target records, while its full-covariance version at rank 128 loses 0.27, and the linear C2ST it drives to 0.5000 is read at 1.0000 by the gradient-boosted detector. Rescaling coordinates helps; nothing about the distributions has been aligned. That is the shape of everything that follows: the operations that move distances or rescale coordinates leave the two representations as distinguishable as before, so the productive question is not how to make the distributions match but what information the latent carries about territory in each domain, which is where the audit of \S\ref{sec:res-audit} and the decoding results of \S\ref{sec:res-decode} take over. \tracenote{Card J CORAL block (+0.0645 +/- 0.020, 3 ckpt x 5 seeds; saturation at 50; full CORAL r=128 -0.27; linear 0.5000 vs GBDT 1.0000; split-half control -0.0008)}

An earlier reading of this comparison was withdrawn, and the mechanism of the error is instructive. At $n=438$ with the \ptbxl-supervised encoder the control led in five of five pre-registered cells, with nominal significance under one mode ($-0.066$, $p=0.040$). \subsection{The circular readout, read against its floor}
\label{sec:floors}

The P2 pipeline reads territory as an angle, and its natural score, the circular agreement \circR, has the constant-predictor floor of \S\ref{sec:p2}: on the four-anchor \ptbxl\ target any constant scores \Rfloorptb. Table~\ref{tab:alpha} shows why that floor has to be printed next to every number. At the regularisation chosen without target labels (generalised cross-validation, $\alpha=741$), the transported latent readout scores $\circR = 0.259$, \emph{below} the floor by $0.033$ ($[-0.061, -0.004]$). Increasing $\alpha$ raises \circR\ steadily, but by concentrating the predictions: the arc covered by the four predicted territory means shrinks from $199^\circ$ toward $47^\circ$, the anatomical ordering breaks, and in the limit ($\alpha = 10^{7}$) a numerically constant predictor still posts a nominally positive margin over the empirically estimated floor ($+0.0013$, interval excluding zero). A metric that a constant can win is not evidence of transfer, and no label-free rule finds the $\alpha$ window that clears the floor legitimately. \tracenote{tmp_t4_alpha_grid.txt medalonly/target rows (GCV 740.6: 0.2593, -0.0328 [-0.0610,-0.0038], arc 199.1; 1e7: 0.2935, +0.0013 [+0.0007,+0.0020], arc 0.6, S(0)=0.9997); MR A.2}

Marginal realism does not predict any of this. If distributional mismatch explained the informativeness gaps, the features with the largest standardised mean differences between domains would show the largest \etasq\ gaps. The correlation between $|\mathrm{SMD}|$ and the absolute gap is $-0.28$ ($p=0.037$) taken at face value, and it is not robust: removing the twelve T-amplitude features flips it to $+0.08$ ($p=0.61$), and a block-level permutation gives $p=0.16$. The honest summary is dissociation. The T block has the worst marginals in the feature set ($|\mathrm{SMD}|$ 1.2--2.5) and is the only informativeness-matched block (\etasq\ 0.032 simulated, 0.028 real); the Q and R blocks have mostly unremarkable marginals ($|\mathrm{SMD}| < 1$) and hide the largest deficits. A validation protocol that checks distributions feature by feature would pass the features that fail this audit, and flag the ones that pass it. \tracenote{FA/f1_fidelity.json→marginal_vs_informativeness (-0.2840, p 0.0374); verifier lines 54-57 (excl-T +0.0828 p 0.607; block-perm p 0.1578); DD C6 (T |SMD| 1.2-2.5)}

Three confounds are declared where they bite. The three simulated interval durations (QRS, QT, P) are missing for 21--24\,\% of simulator rows, and the missingness depends on territory ($\chi^2$ $p$ down to $3.7\times10^{-18}$). Their simulated \etasq\ is therefore treated as unreliable and flagged in every table. In the real cohort, interval missingness is unrelated to territory ($p = 0.34$--$0.94$) and the per-lead blocks are close to complete, with at most 38 of 4,324 rows missing. \texttt{T\_amplitude\_mV} duplicates \texttt{T\_amp\_II} exactly in both domains and is counted once. Repeating the whole audit on the two-class collapse of the label, which removes most of the granularity mismatch between the two label constructions, reproduces the classification of every confirmed feature except one: R\_amp\_V6 is real-informative at four classes ($+0.041$) and simulator-leaning at two ($-0.017$), both intervals excluding zero, and is reported as the exception. \tracenote{FA/f1_fidelity.json→missingness (finite 4968/5089/5168 of 6547; chi2 3.75e-18; real p 0.34-0.94), addendum→duplicate_columns; features R_amp_V6 (+0.0413 [0.027,0.056] / -0.0165 [-0.022,-0.011])}

The strongest objection to the audit is that it compares \etasq\ across two label spaces that differ in kind: the simulated territory is derived from a continuous angle, the real one from diagnosis codes, and \etasq\ is sensitive to class balance. Three observations answer it. First, the map's predictions are tested out of sample in the next two sections by experiments that never compare \etasq\ across domains; readouts are fitted on simulated labels and scored on real rows, and they obey the map. A labelling artefact inside the \etasq\ comparison cannot reach into a transport experiment. Second, the two-class collapse just described shrinks the granularity mismatch and changes one feature of the confirmed set. Third, the flagship axis gap is sixty-five-fold at matched dispersion, with the real-side direction physiologically correct; no plausible class-balance perturbation spans two orders of magnitude. \tracenote{MR Part C.1 / DD Section 1 (objection and answer); sections 4.5-4.6 below}

The shape of the map has a natural clinical reading. The simulator's MI model is an acute ischaemia model: its territory signal lives in the ST segment, the acute injury current. The real cohort is dominated by established infarction (90.2\,\% of the graded records are Stadium II--III or III), whose localisation signature is the Q wave, R-wave loss and the frontal axis. The audit's blind spots and spurious channels are exactly the two sides of that contrast, and I read the map as consistent with an acute-versus-chronic account. It stops short of establishing it: if chronicity were the whole story, transfer should improve on the acute minority of real records, and it does not. Aligning the transported readout's per-record agreement against acuity rank gives $\rho=-0.034$ ($p=0.10$) under the \texttt{source} scaler and $\rho=+0.017$ ($p=0.74$, wrong sign) under \texttt{target}, non-monotone in both, on the 1,490 gradable patients. The acute stratum there is small (61 Stadium~II records), so the test is weak, but it does not support the mechanism, and I do not claim it. \tracenote{CG/floor_audit.json→acuity_strata.acuity_trend (rho -0.0337 p_one_sided 0.0970 / +0.0170 p 0.7431, n_patients 1490); acuity counts Table 3.2 (1617 of 4324, 90.23 pct); claim strength: L in 03_claim_evidence_map.md}

The dataset's own validation saw the symptom of all this without the location. \citet{gillette2023medalcare} validated \medalcare\ by overlaying feature distributions on a real reference and by a clinician Turing test, in which cardiologists diagnosed the pathology of real ECGs at 62\,\% and of simulated ones at 39\,\%. A drop of that size is what one expects if the channels clinicians read for diagnosis carry less information in the simulated signal, which is what the audit measures feature by feature. Their published check of feature distributions was qualitative and class-level, with hand-picked canonical features per pathology; nothing in it could have localised the deficit, and nothing in it did. The audit is the instrument their validation lacked, not a contradiction of it. \tracenote{gillette2023medalcare (62 vs 39 pct, Fig 6 protocol); framing per fence #25}

The audit needs the feature matrix and the labels of both cohorts, and nothing else: no encoder, no training, no transfer experiment. On this data it runs in minutes on a CPU, and every entry comes with a bootstrap interval.

Whether its map means anything for a downstream model is an empirical question. Section~\ref{sec:res-predict} tests the map as a prediction of which channels survive transport; Section~\ref{sec:res-repair} tests whether anything downstream can substitute for the information the map says is absent. \tracenote{instrument character: MR C.4; scripts analysis/fidelity_audit.py (CPU, seeds in header)}

The block orderings carry the statistics. The prediction was written down before the transfer numbers existed. The script that runs this experiment records, ahead of its own results, that \STblock\ would transfer worst and \Qblock\ and \Rblock\ best, on the stated ground that the real domain carries territory where the simulator does not. Ranking the five blocks, simulated informativeness anti-ranks \texttt{source} efficiency at $\rho=-0.90$. With only five blocks, though, the exact one-sided permutation $p$ is 0.042, the two-sided value 0.083 misses 0.05, and the correlation is fragile to the cross-validation seed: a re-run under a different fold seed gives $-0.70$ (one-sided $p=0.117$). Diagonal recalibration erases the rank correlation ($\rho=+0.10$) and lifts \STblock\ from the floor to 0.230 (efficiency 0.296), yet \STblock\ remains second-worst of the five blocks after recalibration. Per-column rescaling repairs the part of the failure that is scale mismatch; it cannot supply territory information the channel does not carry in the target domain. The prediction is therefore a claim about raw transport, and both modes are reported wherever a transport number appears. \qone{Which mode is the primary reading of a transport claim is a declared open question (\S\ref{sec:transport-method}).} \tracenote{FA/f2_blocks.json→spearman (target rho +0.10); efficiency ST 0.006→0.296; ST remains 2nd-worst under target (0.230 < Q 0.241 < globals/T/full54)}

Fitting on the simulator can destroy a channel that works without fitting. The two R-amplitude columns that define the frontal axis, given to the same ridge readout and fitted on \medalcare, transport at 0.175--0.178 under the two modes; they reach only 0.175 in-domain, barely above the 0.126 floor, because the simulator has almost no axis information to fit (\S\ref{sec:res-audit}). The same two measurements used with no fitting at all, by assigning each real record's raw axis angle to the nearest territory anchor, score 0.304, and a readout fitted on \ptbxl\ itself reaches 0.300. The information is in the channel and the anchor frame; what the simulator adds is a coefficient vector pointing at the wrong thing. \tracenote{FA/f2_blocks.json→per_block.axis2 (0.1751/0.1747/0.1778); f3_repair.json→axis_reconciliation naive 0.30431; real-fitted CV 0.2997 MR C.2}

The audit also makes a positive prediction, and it holds. The T block is the one block the audit scores as informativeness-matched (\etasq\ 0.032 simulated, 0.028 real), and after recalibration it is the best-transferring single block, at 0.301 within 0.012 of the full 54-feature set (paired $p=0.22$). Where the simulator writes territory information into a channel at the strength reality does, that channel transfers. \tracenote{FA/f2_blocks.json→per_block.T_amp cross_target 0.3012; paired_cross cross_target:full54_vs_T_amp +0.0115 [-0.0069,+0.0293] p 0.221}

The map, then, predicts raw transport at block level, in both directions. What it cannot say is whether the deficit it measures can be worked around downstream, with the latent in hand: that is the next section's question. \tracenote{bridge}

\section[The limits of repair]{What transfers, what does not, and why: the limits of repair}
\label{sec:res-repair}
% v0 DRAFT 22 Aug (D3). Numbers verified against f3_repair.json + tmp_v_f3-channel-repair_out.txt before writing.
% Paragraph plan: 01_thesis_structure_v3.md §4.6. Claim rows: P6, P3 in 03_claim_evidence_map.md.

If the simulator's territory signal lives in the wrong channels, an obvious repair suggests itself: restrict the latent readout to the channels the audit certifies as faithful. Figure~\ref{fig:repair} and Table~\ref{tab:f3} show what happens. A ridge map from the latent to the 54 features, fitted on \medalcare\ only, defines for each block a low-dimensional representation through which the readout is forced; \ptbxl\ features are never used. Restricted to the Q and R channels (24 dimensions), the readout scores 0.285 cross-domain against 0.340 for the unrestricted 1024-dimensional readout, a paired difference of $-0.055$ (interval $[-0.075, -0.037]$, $p<0.001$) under the \texttt{target} mode. The audit's ordering survives inside the failure: Q+R beats ST by $+0.086$ ($p<0.001$; $+0.078$ under \texttt{source}). Knowing which channels are faithful does not buy transfer, because restriction can only discard information, and what the faithful channels carry is already inside the 1024 dimensions. One restriction reaches parity, and it is worth describing precisely because of what it is. The twelve latent dimensions predicting the four measures in the three inferior leads (II, III, aVF) score 0.343 against the unrestricted 0.340 ($+0.003$, interval $[-0.018, +0.024]$, $p=0.79$; under \texttt{source} $-0.003$, $p=0.76$). This cell passes the norm-matched random-projection null ($p=0.005$; an independent re-implementation gives $p=0.0099$, and both survive a Bonferroni correction over the eight arms tested) and the shuffled-label refit null ($p=0.005$, though that null is degenerate here and certifies only that the readout uses the simulated angle). More tellingly, it beats a readout on the \emph{measured} inferior-lead features by $+0.044$ ($[+0.022, +0.066]$, $p<0.001$; the measured features alone score 0.300), so the latent's twelve inferior-lead dimensions carry something the extracted features do not. It is the best of eight arms and is reported as parity under that selection, not as an improvement: everything the 1024-dimensional latent transfers about territory fits in twelve dimensions aimed at the inferior leads. \tracenote{FA/f3_repair.json→blocks.inferior 0.3427, paired.inferior_vs_unrestricted_{target,source}, nulls.inferior_randproj p 0.00498 (verifier 0.0099), inferior_shufphi p 0.005 degenerate (null mass at 0.1366); verifier competitor 0.2995 delta +0.0441 [+0.0219,+0.0658]}

The falsification arm asks whether the deficit is covariate shift after all: if the simulator carries the right information at the wrong input distribution, reweighting its training rows toward the real feature distribution should help. It hurts. Weighting \medalcare\ rows by a domain classifier on the two axis-defining features and refitting, transfer drops by $-0.073$ ($[-0.086, -0.061]$, $p<0.001$) against the unweighted readout on the same rows. The effective sample size falls to 1,120 of 6,513. A control that resamples uniformly to the same effective size scores 0.329 against the weighted 0.273, so the harm is the weighting itself, not the lost rows. A six-feature weighting summary harms by a similar amount ($-0.069$, $p<0.001$), but its effective sample size collapses to 208 of 4,968 and most of that harm is attributable to the collapse, so I rest the conclusion on the axis-pair arm. Giving more weight to the rows of the simulator that look most like reality moves the readout away from what reality needs. That is what one expects when the missing ingredient is information rather than distribution. \tracenote{FA/f3_repair.json→reweight.axpair (-0.0732 [-0.0859,-0.0609], ESS 1120/6513), reweight.six (-0.0690, ESS 208/4968); verifier ESS-matched control 0.3287 vs weighted 0.2734; caveat per forbidden #21}

The sensitivity encoder reproduces the pattern: on the dual-head encoder the Q+R restriction loses by $-0.064$ and the inferior-lead cell sits at $+0.0004$ ($p=0.96$). \tracenote{FA/f3_repair.json→exp8_leadfix_dual.paired}

Taken together, the three experiments say the deficit is missing information, not misplaced information. The audit measures an absence in the synthetic signal; transport tracks what the channels carry in reality and not what the simulator believes about them; and no downstream operation, not channel selection, not recalibration, not reweighting, substitutes for what was never written into the waveform. What remains after every repair is a representation-level fact: the two-dimensional planes from which territory is read out in the two domains are close to orthogonal in the latent space (normalised principal-angle overlap 0.008, interval $[0.002, 0.017]$, against split-half ceilings near 0.31--0.36), even though local neighbourhood structure corresponds in part (a nearest-neighbour transfer reaches 32--42\,\% of its ceiling). The latent is not unrelated across domains; the linear directions that encode territory are. \tracenote{2026-08-12 tier1 §2.4 (normalised overlap 0.0076 [0.0016,0.0168], ceilings 0.360/0.311) and §2.5 (kNN 32-42 pct of ceiling), both survive the 08-13 audit (MR C.4); dossier C Card I}

What does transfer is small, coherent and identifiable: a twelve-dimensional inferior-lead subspace at parity with the full latent, and a zero-shot anchor frame that the axis reaches without any fitting. Both point the same way. The transferable content is the part of the simulator's territory encoding that overlaps with where reality writes territory, and enlarging it is not an encoder problem. A simulator that wrote territory into the Q and R waves and the frontal axis at the strength the real cohort shows would give a readout something to carry; measuring exactly which channels need that correction is what the audit is for. \tracenote{P3/P6 composition; MR C.4}
