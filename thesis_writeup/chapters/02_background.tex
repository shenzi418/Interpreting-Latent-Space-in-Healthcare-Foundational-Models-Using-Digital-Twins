\section{The 12-lead ECG and infarct localisation}
\label{sec:bg-ecg}

The 12-lead electrocardiogram measures the heart's electrical activity along twelve fixed spatial directions: six frontal-plane limb leads and six horizontal-plane precordial leads. Because the leads are directions, the location of a pathology maps onto \emph{which} leads express it, and a century of clinical practice reads infarct location that way: inferior infarcts show in leads II, III and aVF, anterior and septal ones in V1--V4, lateral ones in I, aVL, V5 and V6. The oldest summary of the frontal-plane information is the mean QRS axis, a single angle computed from two lead amplitudes, which shifts superiorly when inferior myocardium stops conducting. \tracenote{standard electrocardiography; no claim beyond textbook content}

Which waveform component carries the localisation depends on the age of the infarct. In the acute phase the injured tissue sustains a current of injury that deviates the ST segment, and the universal definition of myocardial infarction reads acute ischaemia from ST elevation and depression in specified lead groups \citep{thygesen2018fourth}. Established infarction expresses itself differently: necrotic tissue is electrically silent, producing pathological Q waves and loss of R-wave amplitude in the leads facing it, and the same definition reads prior infarction from Q-wave criteria. The two signatures are not interchangeable, and they do not persist equally: Q waves themselves can regress over years \citep{das2006significance}. What this thesis takes from clinical electrocardiography is the expectation that \emph{where} territory information sits in the waveform is a property of the cohort's acuity mix, so a simulator of acute ischaemia and a registry of chronic infarcts may both encode territory while encoding it in different measurements.

\section{Cardiac digital twins and MedalCare-XL}
\label{sec:bg-twin}

A cardiac digital twin, in the sense used here, is a mechanistic electrophysiological simulation of a human heart in a torso, detailed enough that a 12-lead ECG can be computed from it. Anatomy, conduction and cellular electrophysiology are parameterised; pathology is introduced by changing parameters, for infarction the location, extent and transmurality of an ischaemic region; and every simulated recording therefore comes with its generating parameters known exactly. That property is what makes twins attractive as training data: labels that no clinical registry can provide, such as the precise angular position of an infarct, are free.

\medalcare~\citep{gillette2023medalcare} is the largest public realisation of this idea: 16,839 simulated 12-lead ECGs across eight pathology classes, produced from whole-heart simulations with varied anatomies and electrophysiological parameters (\S\ref{sec:data-medalcare}). Its authors validated the dataset in two ways. They overlaid distributions of standard ECG features on a real reference cohort, class by class, with hand-picked canonical features for each pathology; and they ran a clinician Turing test, in which cardiologists distinguished simulated from real healthy ECGs at 77\,\% and diagnosed the pathology of real ECGs at 62\,\% against 39\,\% for simulated ones. Both checks are informative, and neither is feature-resolved: the distributional check was qualitative and class-level, and the Turing test measures a deficit without saying where in the signal it lives. What the thesis takes from \medalcare's own validation is its open question: the 62-versus-39 gap says simulated pathology carries less diagnosable information than real pathology, and nothing published locates that missing information in the waveform. The audit of Chapter~\ref{ch:contribution} is built to answer exactly that form of question. \tracenote{gillette2023medalcare figures per MR D.2 (77.33/83/62 vs 39; Fig 6 protocol); fence #25 wording}

\section{PTB-XL}
\label{sec:bg-ptbxl}

\ptbxl~\citep{wagner2020ptbxl} is the reference public clinical ECG corpus: 21,799 ten-second 12-lead recordings from 18,869 patients, annotated with SCP diagnostic statements, aggregated into five superclasses, and shipped with a patient-stratified ten-fold split that the community uses for benchmarking \citep{strodthoff2021deep}.

Its myocardial-infarction statements are sub-typed by location (anterior, anteroseptal, anterolateral, inferior, inferolateral, lateral, posterior), which is what allows a four-class territory label to be constructed for this thesis (\S\ref{sec:data-ptbxl}). A coarse acuity staging exists for a minority of MI records; among the graded records, 90\,\% are Stadium II--III or III. The thesis takes two facts from \ptbxl: territory labels must be constructed from its diagnosis codes, and its MI cohort is dominated by established infarction, which is the cohort property \S\ref{sec:bg-ecg} says should matter. \tracenote{counts Table 3.2; acuity A§1.2.6}

\section{ECG foundation models}
\label{sec:bg-fm}

Foundation models for the ECG are encoders trained on very large recording corpora whose internal representations are then reused, frozen or lightly adapted, for downstream tasks. \ecgfounder~\citep{li2024ecgfounder} is trained on over ten million clinician-annotated recordings and released with weights, which is why it is the backbone here (\S\ref{sec:encoder}). Interpretability work on such models is recent and, so far, real-data-only: dictionary-learning and sparse-autoencoder approaches decompose ECG foundation representations into clinically nameable components \citep{duan2026cadence,duan2026interpbench}, asking what a latent dimension means in terms of clinical concepts. None of this line asks the question posed here, whether the latent can be interpreted in the \emph{mechanistic} parameters of a simulator, nor what happens to such an interpretation across the synthetic-to-real boundary. That unoccupied position, to our knowledge, is where this thesis stands. \tracenote{fence D; searches MR D.1}

\section{Inverse inference of infarct parameters}
\label{sec:bg-inverse}

Recovering infarct properties from the ECG is a form of the inverse problem of electrocardiography, and its deep-learning instantiations are advancing quickly on simulated data. \citet{li2024tmi} infer infarct location and size from simulated QRS complexes within a cardiac digital-twin framework; the same group's survey \citep{li2025survey} reports localisation quality around Dice 0.4 and is explicit that clinical translation remains open, since paired real ECGs with ground-truth infarct geometry barely exist. Simulation-trained models tested on real recordings do exist for other localisation tasks: pacing-site prediction for outflow-tract arrhythmias transfers from simulated to clinical ECGs \citep{doste2022training}, as does atrial-fibrillation driver localisation \citep{luongo2021machine}, and simulated ischaemia has been used to design features later applied to real signals \citep{ledezma2019modeling}. For infarct territory from a foundation-model latent, the simulated-to-real step is, to our knowledge, unclosed. This thesis does not close it. It measures the gap, explains it at feature level, and shows which part of it no downstream method crosses; the distinction between measuring and closing is kept explicit throughout. \tracenote{fences C, F; MR D.1-D.2}

\section{The domain gap and alignment}
\label{sec:bg-align}

Synthetic-to-real transfer is a domain-adaptation problem, and the standard toolkit appears throughout Chapter~\ref{ch:results}. Maximum mean discrepancy \citep{gretton2012kernel} measures distributional distance in a kernel space and doubles as a training penalty, including class-conditional variants; iterative nullspace projection \citep{ravfogel2020null} removes the directions a linear classifier uses to separate two groups; correlation alignment in its diagonal form rescales coordinates on second-order statistics \citep{sun2016coral}. Whether two distributions have actually been brought together is conventionally judged by a classifier two-sample test: train a classifier to tell the domains apart and read its held-out AUROC, with chance meaning indistinguishable \citep{lopezpaz2017c2st}. The caution the thesis attaches to this toolkit is the one \S\ref{sec:res-align} demonstrates: the detector inherits the capacity of its classifier, so a linear C2ST at chance licenses only the claim that no \emph{linear} separation remains, and decisions taken on such readings can be wrong in both directions. \tracenote{used in §4.2; instrument lesson}

\section{Evaluating synthetic data beyond marginal realism}
\label{sec:bg-syntheval}

How to tell whether synthetic data is good enough to use is a literature of its own, and its instruments have grown steadily more demanding. The oldest practice is distributional: overlay feature marginals and judge similarity. Train-on-synthetic-test-on-real protocols made utility measurable \citep{esteban2017real}, and evaluation frameworks for synthetic health data now standardise fidelity and utility metrics of several kinds \citep{goncalves2020generation,alaa2021faithful}. Closer to this thesis are agreement-based instruments: whether analyses run on synthetic data reach the conclusions of the same analyses on the real data \citep{elemam2024replicability}, and whether models trained on a synthetic replica rank feature importances as the real-data model does \citep{hudovernik2024benchmarking,ortuno2026highfidelity}. In parallel, the simulation community assesses mechanistic models through verification, validation and uncertainty quantification frameworks oriented at regulatory credibility \citep{morrison2019credibility,viceconti2021insilico,pathmanathan2024credibility}.

Two gaps in this literature matter here. The agreement-based instruments all evaluate generative \emph{replicas} of a cohort against the same cohort; none audits a \emph{mechanistic simulator} against an independent real population, where disagreement can reflect physiology rather than generator error. And none of the instruments connects a feature-level fidelity measurement to a transfer prediction that is then tested. The audit of \S\ref{sec:audit-method}, which compares per-feature label-informativeness between a simulator and an unrelated real cohort and validates the comparison against transport experiments, occupies that intersection, to our knowledge, alone. \tracenote{fences A, E; nearest neighbours credited per MR D.1}

\section{Circular statistics for angular readouts}
\label{sec:bg-circ}

Reading territory as an angle imports the statistics of circular data, along with that literature's known traps. Agreement between predicted and true angles is naturally summarised by the mean resultant length of the errors, but resultant-length statistics are biased wherever the underlying distribution is non-uniform: phase-clustering bias inflates coupling measures \citep{vandriel2015phase}, non-zero circular means produce spurious phase locking \citep{kovach2017biased}, and correlation coefficients built to be immune to such artefacts exist precisely because the naive statistic is not \citep{fisherlee1983correlation,jammalamadaka1988correlation}. Two further cautions apply directly. Fitting circular-linear associations by maximising a resultant length has a non-unique optimum on the cylinder, so slopes recovered that way are not identifiable \citep{kempter2012quantifying}; and empirical chance levels in classification depart from theoretical ones badly enough at realistic sample sizes that decoding claims need explicit nulls \citep{combrisson2015exceeding}. The thesis's constant-predictor floor (\S\ref{sec:p2}) belongs to this family of corrections: it states the exact supremum a label-independent predictor attains on a non-uniform angular target, and shows that permutation nulls sit below it.

\section{Negative results as measurements}
\label{sec:bg-negative}

There is an established register for work whose central finding is that something widely relied upon does not do what is assumed, and its best examples share a structure: state the belief fairly, measure the failure with a named mechanism, and leave behind an instrument. Chest-radiograph models that generalise poorly because they learn hospital signatures \citep{zech2018variable}; COVID classifiers that select shortcuts, shown by explicit spurious-signal analysis \citep{degrave2021shortcuts}; hidden stratification as a measurable failure class \citep{oakdenrayner2020hidden}; saliency methods that fail sanity checks \citep{adebayo2018sanity}; the theoretical impossibility of unsupervised disentanglement without inductive bias \citep{locatello2018challenging}; the systematic review finding no benefit of machine learning over logistic regression in clinical prediction \citep{christodoulou2019systematic}; and the argument that current explainability offers false reassurance in clinical use \citep{ghassemi2021false}. This thesis is written in that register. Its negative findings are presented as measurements with mechanisms, its audit as the reusable instrument, and its positives, the in-domain interpretation and the partial transfer, stand alongside them rather than being displaced by them.
