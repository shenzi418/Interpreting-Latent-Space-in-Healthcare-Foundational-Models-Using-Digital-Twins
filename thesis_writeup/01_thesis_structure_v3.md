## Ch1 Introduction (4 pp, ≈ 12 ¶)
1.1 Motivation (3 ¶). ¶1 Cardiac digital twins produce labelled 12-lead ECGs at scale with the generating parameters known; ECG foundation models produce reusable representations; putting them together promises readouts of mechanistic quantities (infarct territory, size, transmurality) that no clinical label set provides. ¶2 The assumption this rests on, stated fairly: a synthetic cohort validated for distributional realism (feature marginals; clinician Turing test) is a usable training/validation cohort for a downstream model. Gillette 2023 validated MedalCare-XL that way; the Turing test's 39 % vs 62 % pathology diagnosability is quoted as the authors' own measurement (Q4c). ¶3 What is at stake for the field (one paragraph, no drama): if that assumption fails in a way marginal realism cannot see, every simulator-trained readout inherits the failure silently.
1.2 The question (3 ¶). ¶4 As registered: can the latent space of an ECG FM adapted to a digital twin be interpreted in the twin's parameters θ, and does the interpretation carry to real ECG. ¶5 As it developed: the in-domain answer, the cross-domain shortfall under a pre-registered rule, and the question the shortfall forces — *why*. ¶6 The approach in one paragraph (Fig 3.1 in words): one frozen encoder, two cohorts, three questions, one instrument.

## Ch2 Background (8 pp; each subsection ends with one sentence "what this thesis takes from it")
2.1 ECG and infarct localisation (3 ¶): the 12-lead vectorcardiographic logic (frontal-plane leads and the QRS axis); acute injury current → ST deviation vs established necrosis → Q waves and R loss (Thygesen 2018 criteria); Q-wave regression over years (Das 2006) → why a chronic-dominated cohort writes territory into Q/R. Take-away: territory information can sit in different measure-blocks depending on acuity.
2.2 Digital twins and MedalCare-XL (4 ¶): mechanistic simulation pipeline (anatomies, EP parameters, forward ECG); the release (16,839; 8 classes; MI by occlusion/transmurality); the parameters θ; **its validation, quoted fairly**: per-lead marginal overlays vs PTB-XL+ (Fig 6 hand-picked class-level features), Turing test 77.33 %/83 %/62-vs-39. Take-away: validation was distributional and clinician-level; nothing per-feature, sub-pathology, or transfer-linked (fence #25).
2.3 PTB-XL (2 ¶): cohort, SCP statements, folds, MI sub-labels, `infarction_stadium`; Strodthoff benchmarks. Take-away: the real MI cohort is chronic-dominated and territory must be derived from statements.
2.4 ECG foundation models (3 ¶): ECGFounder (Li 2024/2025); adapters/linear probing on frozen backbones; the interpretability lane is real-data only (CADENCE, ECG-InterpBench). Take-away: no prior FM-latent interpretation against simulator parameters.
2.5 Inverse inference of infarct parameters (2 ¶): in-silico state of the art (Li TMI 2024; RBME 2025 Dice ~0.4); sim-trained/real-tested localisation for other tasks (Doste 2022 OT-VA; Luongo 2021 AF drivers). Take-away: the sim→real step for infarct/θ from an FM latent is unclosed; this thesis measures and explains the gap, does not close it (fence F).
2.6 Domain gap and alignment (3 ¶): MMD and its class-conditional form; INLP; C2ST as the detector; CORAL/AdaBN as recalibration. Take-away: the tools §4.2 uses, and their known blind spot (a linear detector licenses only linear claims).
2.7 Evaluating synthetic data beyond marginals (4 ¶): TSTR (Esteban); utility frameworks (Goncalves, Alaa, El Emam); feature-importance agreement on same-cohort replicas (Hudovernik; Ortuno); DT credibility/V&V (Morrison; Pathmanathan; Viceconti); simulation-driven feature design (Ledezma). Take-away: none audits per-feature label informativeness of a mechanistic simulator against a real cohort or links it to transfer — the slot the audit fills (fence A/E, "to our knowledge").
2.8 Circular statistics for angular readouts (2 ¶): resultant length and its bias (phase-clustering bias van Driel; nonzero-mean spurious locking Kovach); circular correlation (Fisher & Lee; Jammalamadaka & Sarma 1988); arg-max non-uniqueness (Kempter 2012); chance-level caution (Combrisson & Jerbi). Take-away: R̄ needs a constant baseline; the thesis states it as a floor and shows the permutation null sits below it (Part B; monograph caveat: not found in the standard references consulted, no priority claim).
2.9 Negative results as measurements (1 ¶): Zech, DeGrave, Oakden-Rayner, Christodoulou, Ghassemi — the register the thesis adopts: fair statement of the belief, negative as measurement with mechanism, reusable instrument.

### 4.1 Adapting ECGFounder to the digital twin (2 pp, 5 ¶)
¶1 [Table 4.1: exp8 family test macro-F1/AUC per domain, from `metrics.json`] The adapted encoders classify well in-domain: MedalCare 0.91–0.95, PTB-XL 0.82–0.83 for jointly trained runs (→A§2.3.2). ¶2 The MedalCare-only encoder reaches 0.927 on MedalCare and 0.457 on PTB-XL as a passive readout — a class-level transfer number that frames everything after (P2 territory transfer is a harder question than class transfer). ¶3 Bottleneck K=64 loses nothing (0.952 / 0.832). ¶4 The 2×2 design (shared vs dual head × ±ccMMD): with the validated pipeline the four exp8 arms are within 0.01 in-domain; the pre-fix observation that the shared head raised cross-domain class transfer (LR M→P AUC ~0.59→~0.76) is reported in Appendix A.2 as pre-fix and its survival is stated as untested (audit: UNKNOWN). ¶5 Consequence: encoders differ little in what they classify; the differences that matter are in what the latent carries (→ 4.3) and never in the encoders as replicates (~2 effective observations).

### 4.2 The domain gap in the latent space: what it is made of, and why the standard detector misses it (3.5 pp, 8 ¶)
¶1 [Table 4.2: three-condition table — swapped / validated / validated+per-lead: C2ST 1.0000/1.0000/1.0000; MMD 0.1757→0.1447; kNN mixing 0.0026→0.0109] The two latent distributions are separable by a held-out linear domain classifier in every configuration; correcting the loader moves distance statistics but not separability (→P6′; C Card G). ¶2 Four alignment attempts (training-time MMD/ccMMD; post-hoc INLP at K=1024 and low K; PCA to K=8; trained bottleneck K=16) leave held-out C2ST ≈1.0; ccMMD reduces the MMD statistic 8–13 % and changes nothing downstream (one sentence each; magnitudes appendix). ¶3 [Table 4.3: over-determination, 6 rows] The gap is over-determined: marginals alone 0.9999, dependence structure alone 0.9993, floor 0.5032, six checkpoints down to K=16; the K16 pair (linear 0.58 vs GBDT 0.997 on dependence alone) shows what a linear detector misses. ¶4 The withdrawn frontier: an INLP run that stopped at its iteration budget produced a linear C2ST near chance; a gradient-boosted C2ST on the same latents read 0.9999 — the rule adopted: a linear probe licenses only claims about linear structure. ¶5 What the loader correction did to territory transfer, as an instrument test: the involution 2×2 (as shipped 0.2132 p=0.756 → corrected 0.3278 p=0.0020, sign-reversal interaction), stated as consistent with the fix, never as proof of it. ¶6 [Table 4.4: sweep summary; 0/66 transpositions detected; 39/66 score above identity; identity itself p=0.1139 → transfer is a corruption detector, not a lead-order detector; C2ST is blind to gross corruption (→P6″). ¶7 Diagonal CORAL as the one alignment-side positive: M→P class transfer +0.0645±0.020 (3 ckpt × 5 seeds), saturating at 50 unlabelled target samples; linear C2ST 0.5000 while GBDT stays 1.0000 — calibration, not alignment (C Card J). ¶8 Consequence: distribution-level alignment is not the route; the question becomes what information the latent carries about territory and whether it is the same information in both domains (→ 4.3, 4.4).

4.3.2 The pre-registered rule (3 ¶). ¶4 [Table 4.6: rule + verdict, both modes] The rule, quoted verbatim with provenance (May log; script constant), scored on the MedalCare-only encoder, n=4324: 4-class 0.3440 (`target`) / 0.3357 (`target_pool_measured`) vs ≥0.45 — POSITIVE not met; 2-class 0.6299 / 0.6521 vs ≥0.55 — PARTIAL met under both modes (footnote: 0.65 in later notes → `target_pool_measured` only). ¶5 Permutation p at the 1e-4 floor for both arms on both endpoints: certifies label use, orders nothing. ¶6 The reading: real ECGs are partly localised (anterior vs inferior) by a readout that never saw a real label; the finer four-way distinction is not achieved.
4.3.3 Latent vs control across the gap (3 ¶). ¶7 [Table 4.7: the twelve paired cells] Statistically indistinguishable on the 4-class primary (+0.0145 p=0.162 / +0.0041 p=0.695 at n=4324); one 2-class hit +0.0195 p=0.044 and one opposite hit; neither survives Holm (→P2′). ¶8 What the control is (54 features; instrument checks; 24.7 % imputed rows in the real matrix; imputation confound checked in the control's favour). ¶9 Why the earlier reading of this comparison (control ahead 5/5 at n=438) was withdrawn: it required an encoder that had been supervised on PTB-XL; with the MedalCare-only encoder and 9.9× rows the difference vanishes; the control reproduces to four decimals across encoders so only the latent moved (encoder +0.033/+0.040; eval-set contribution inside the n=438 CI).
